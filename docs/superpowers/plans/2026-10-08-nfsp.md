# Neural Fictitious Self-Play Implementation Plan

**Goal:** NFSP (Heinrich & Silver 2016) for Kuhn and Leduc with a `paper` and a `dream` preset, fast enough for
3e6-iteration Leduc runs, and the report tables against both published curves.

**Architecture:** One new module, `headsup/algos/nfsp.py`: numpy networks with a hand-written backward pass (the torch
modules of the same architecture provide the initial weights and are the reference in the tests), index-based
memories, a solver that plays 128 tables in one vectorised pass over the compiled tree (`Tree` of
`headsup/algos/pdcfr.py`) and a CLI with checkpoints. `headsup/algos/leduc_report.py` gets three tables.

**Tech Stack:** Python 3.12, numpy, PyTorch (initialisation, checkpoint files, test references), pytest.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-nfsp-design.md`

## Review Focus

1. **Seat symmetry**: the reward of seat 1 is the negated utility; each seat's next state is its own next infoset, not
   the opponent's; the seat that did not make the last action still gets its terminal transition.
   Tests: `test_every_transition_reaches_m_rl_for_both_seats`, `test_q_learning_finds_the_best_response_to_a_fixed_opponent`.
2. **Hand boundaries**: pending decisions must not leak into the next hand of a table; modes are redrawn exactly there.
   Tests: the same, and `test_mode_is_drawn_per_hand_and_seat_and_held`.
3. **Hand-written gradients**: the dueling head, the normalisation and the skip connections.
   Tests: `test_network_step_is_sgd_with_gradient_norm_clipping`, `test_updates_equal_torch_autograd`.
4. **Stale forward caches**: Double DQN evaluates the online network at the next states before the pass whose
   activations the backward pass uses. Test: `test_updates_equal_torch_autograd` (`dream` cases).
5. **Snapshots**: `state_dict()` copies; `load_state_dict()` copies; the random generator's state is part of it.
   Test: `test_checkpoint_continues_identically_and_shares_nothing`.
6. **The reward unit**: the `paper` preset divides utilities by 2.6 (the DREAM code's unit); with antes it does not
   reproduce the paper (spec, "The reward unit of the `paper` preset"). The validation runs decide whether that holds.

## Validation runs (task 10)

```bash
mkdir -p runs/leduc_nfsp
for s in 0 1 2; do
  for preset in paper dream; do
    CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="" OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 nohup .venv/bin/python -m headsup.algos.nfsp --game leduc --preset $preset \
      --iterations 3000000 --seed $s --json runs/leduc_nfsp/leduc_${preset}_s$s.json \
      --checkpoint runs/leduc_nfsp/leduc_${preset}_s$s.pt > runs/leduc_nfsp/leduc_${preset}_s$s.log 2>&1 &
  done
done
.venv/bin/python -m headsup.algos.leduc_report
```

Six processes, one core each: `paper` about 1.2 h, `dream` about 8 h (2 500 000 iterations - the end of the DREAM
curve at 3.2e8 nodes - about 6.5 h; 3 000 000 keeps both presets comparable on the paper's axis). An interrupted
command continues from its checkpoint when it is started again. Kuhn: `--game kuhn`, file names
`kuhn_${preset}_s$s`, `--iterations 200000` (minutes). The paper's reading of the rewards: `--reward-scale 1`.

## Measured throughput

Leduc, one thread, 30-40 threads of other jobs on the machine's 32 cores (128 env steps and 8 SGD steps per iteration):

| preset | play (128 steps) | `Q` update | `Pi` update | iteration | env steps / s | SGD updates / s | 3e6 iterations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `paper` | 0.32-0.36 ms | 0.12-0.14 ms | 0.12-0.13 ms | 1.3-1.6 ms | 82 000-99 000 | 5 100-6 200 | 1.1-1.3 h |
| `dream` | 0.9-1.1 ms | 1.1-1.4 ms | 0.7-0.9 ms | 8.0-10.8 ms | 11 800-16 000 | 740-1 000 | 6.7-9.0 h |

For comparison, torch autograd + `torch.optim.SGD` on the same machine: 0.48-0.57 ms per `Q` step for the MLP,
3.5 ms for `game.make_model(arch="deepcfr_dueling")` (Double DQN, clipping), 1.9 ms per `Pi` step.

## Review fix pass

An independent review of the merged branch found no correctness bug in the algorithm and three things to fix:

1. **A resumed run ignored `--seed`**: a seed-0 checkpoint continued with `--seed 1` reproduced the seed-0 curve under
   the other label. The solver keeps its seed, stores it in `state_dict()` and refuses another one on load
   (`state.get("seed", self.seed)`: checkpoints written before are accepted).
2. **The explanation of the reward unit was not supported by the data** (dead hidden units as the cause; "learning
   rate and reward unit are one setting"). The spec now states the choice as a calibration on three seeds and what the
   division is exactly: antes with the first-layer step of `Q` divided by 6.76 and its initial output layer times 2.6.
3. **Nine mutants at the level of the command line and the seed survived the tests.** The curve file was also rewritten
   in place at every evaluation; it is now written under another name and renamed, as the checkpoint.
